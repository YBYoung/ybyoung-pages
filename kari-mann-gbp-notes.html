Kari Mann: how to read the Google profile numbers

Sophia, this is for you, not for the client. Kari sent her Google profile's Performance screen showing 261 interactions for September. Obviously, the situation is more nuanced than that, and we have some good news to share, but it will require proper framing.

It's good that they are trying to stipulate actual GBP performance metrics instead of rank. I consider this progress. But now we need to help them understand how to think about measuring performance change over time. Part of that means they need to see what their performance has looked like over the past year or so (as far back as Google's data goes, at least). 

Kari's "page 2" point, answered directly

Kari's instinct is right about the pressure, and wrong about where it leaves her. Google really has stacked more paid placements above the free map results. On a phone search for "dental implants cape coral" on September 29, four ads filled the first screen and a half before the map results started. But the map results are not the new page 2. They are still the main unpaid spot for a local search. On that implant search and on "best dentist in cape coral", Kari Mann was the first unpaid listing. We also did not see a single Local Services Ad on the ten Cape Coral searches we captured. On desktop, Google dropped infinite scroll in 2024 and went back to page buttons (Search Engine Roundtable, June 25, 2024). On phones, a "More search results" button loads the next set below, which works like a new page without leaving the one you are on. The loss we can measure is on the regular results page on phones. On Maps, the profile was shown 906 times last summer and 982 times this summer. So the job is not escaping page 2. It is turning the visibility she already has into more calls, and that is what the list below is built for.

The short and sweet version

  • The drop in GBP views (measured in impressions) started in November 2025, before they started working with us. It is mostly the profile being shown less on the regular Google results page on mobile. Views on Google Maps are generally flat (if not slightly growing) since we launched the site in May. 
  • When it comes to GBP, calls are the primary Lead Gen Metric. Their calls have been nose-diving since October of last year, and when we launched the site and made our optimizations earlier in the summer, they saw August as one of the two months where the trend truly reversed. I can't say for certain why calls are down for September (aside from the fact that September is not over yet). Seasonality can't be ruled out.

    Looking at the entire Q3 vs Q2 however, we can firmly say that their business profile views are up QoQ by at least 26%. 
    Their calls, however, are down 20-21% QoQ. We can't make their audience call; we can only put the practice in front of more of them to increase the odds of that lead-gen action happening on GBP and on the website. Like I said earlier, August was actually their only other instance of that down-trend reversing, other than March. 

My recommendations: 1) give the priority services their own section on the homepage

I recommend we request this from the dev and design team: a section on the homepage where each of Kari's top-3 priority services gets its spot in that block, with the service name, a very-very short description, and a link to that service's page. I understand if the team does not want to move in that direction, though. 

Why it could help: The homepage is currently one of the main pages the Google profile sends people to directly. The Website button on the profile opens the homepage today. That stays true until the location page (which I recommend adding below) is live and the profile points to it; after that, the homepage is still where most other visitors land.

For those visitors, the homepage is the first thing they see of the practice after the profile. It is also the page Google reads when it decides what the profile is about (because it is the one that is linked to).

What is there now. The homepage's main heading is "Family & Cosmetic Dentist in Cape Coral, FL". The only section that touches on services is the one below, called "Smiles": one paragraph and three buttons. 

My second recommendation: a dedicated location page, and a contact page that better serves their users

I also think we should add a new page just dedicated to talking about the Cape Coral office, and a rewrite of the contact page so it is only about getting in touch. A proper location page does not exist yet.

Then point the Google profile at it. My recommendation is that once the location page is live, the Website link on the Google profile can go to it instead of the homepage. The page should be, in effect, an on-site version of the Google profile: the same name, address, phone, hours, map and some photos, plus links to the priority services Kari wants to promote. 

What is there now. The contact page (karimanndentalstudio.com/contact-us) is doing both jobs. Its main heading is "Visit Kari Mann Dental Studio in Cape Coral, FL", and it has four parts: the address and phone number with Schedule and Directions buttons, a map, a four-field message form, and the hours. It does not name any service, any nearby area, or anything a patient should know before they call/reach out.

The location page should be about the office and where it sits, and match important parts of the Google profile:

  • The address, a map, directions from the surrounding areas Kari's patients come from, parking and entrance details, and photos of the building.
  • The priority services she wants to be found for, each with a short description and a link to its own page.
  • Local detail only this office can claim: the new building on Del Prado Boulevard, the neighborhoods and nearby towns it serves, and landmarks near it.

The contact page becomes about making contact easy:

  • Phone, message form, hours, and how quickly the office replies.
  • Short answers to the questions the front desk hears most, which Kari's team can give us.
  • What to have ready when calling or writing: the reason for the visit, dental or medical history that matters, and payment details, in the office's own words.

On the call: what I would say, and what I would avoid

Say
  • The drop in GBP Performance started in November 2025, before we began and before the new site launched.
  • Calls from the profile have been lower since May. We are measuring that, not guessing at it.
  • We report on GBP Performance metrics like Impressions, Calls, and Website Clicks. Interactions are any kind of interaction a user had on the profile, including clicking on a social profile or looking at a post, but those actions aren't necessarily strong engagement signals.
  • If the devs and design confirm it, you can say we are giving her priority services their own section on the homepage, because that is the page her Google profile currently sends people to and that can indirectly help improve their profile's relevance for those services.
Avoid
  • Promising a date that performance will improve or any concrete projection. Local SEO is largely dependent on local demand. 
  • Naming a cause for the fluctuation in GBP calls since site launch. We do not have one we can support. 
  • Using any single search for their practice as proof of performance/rank. Rank checks should be done using our tool which is able to do an objective view.
