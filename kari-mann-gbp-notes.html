Sophia, this is for you, not for the client. Kari sent the Performance screen from the practice's Google Business Profile showing 261 interactions for September, the lowest month on that chart, as one more sign the trend is heading the wrong way. It covers the Google profile only.

The decline in profile interactions is real, and it is worth watching. It did not start this month or with the new website: it began in November 2025, and most of it is one thing, the profile being shown less often in regular mobile Google search results. Calls held up longer than views did and have been lower since May, which is the part we cannot fully explain yet. Views of the listing on Google Maps itself are flat to slightly up over the same period. September is also not finished: Google's data for the month ends on the 25th, and at the current daily pace the month lands near 320, level with February and June. One part of this is specific to the practice: implant searches. Those searches faded in the fall of 2025 and have started to return since the new site launched.

What Kari said, and what the full record shows

What Kari said

What the data shows

The 261 is real but incomplete. Google's Business Profile data runs several days behind and revises the most recent two to three weeks. When we pulled the profile through Google's API on September 29, the last full day of data was September 25. Through that date the month had 267 interactions, 10.7 a day, which puts the full month near 320. February had 319 and June had 305. September is on the low side, not at a new low.

The decline is older than the website. The chart on her screen starts in April. The full record below starts in April 2025. Interactions ran 430 to 555 a month through October 2025, stepped down in November 2025, and have run 305 to 467 since. The new website went live on May 29, 2026, six months after the step down. The month of the switch (June, 305) was the low point and the three months since have been higher: 344, 397, and September on pace for about 320.

Calls are the part to watch. Views and website clicks stepped down in November 2025. Calls did not: they held between 104 and 156 a month from April 2025 through April 2026, and have run 81 to 99 a month since May. September has 57 through the 25th, 2.3 a day against 3.2 in August, including one week (September 14 to 20) with six calls. We do not yet know why. May is the month the new site launched and the month of a Google core update, and things happening in the same month is not evidence that either one caused it.

Where the drop is: views on Google Search

Google reports two different things for a profile. Impressions are how many times the profile was shown to someone, split by where it was shown: on Google Maps, or in a regular Google Search results page. Interactions are what people did after seeing it. Looking at them separately shows where the loss sits.

What this does and does not tell us. Views fell further than actions did: June to August views were down 39 percent on last year, and calls, website clicks and direction requests together were down 26 percent. So the loss starts with fewer people seeing the profile on Google Search. It does not mean the listing is doing better. Calls are down in plain numbers, 271 this summer against 363 last summer, and that is the number Kari is watching.

How firm Google's numbers are. Calls, website clicks and direction requests are counts of taps and are the firmer numbers. Views are softer: Search Engine Roundtable reported a drop in Search views across many profiles in late June 2025 that a volunteer Google Product Expert called "a known glitch" (July 11, 2025), and September 2026 data was missing until September 11 (September 8, 2026). Read the views chart for its direction, and the calls for their count.

What a Cape Coral searcher sees on Google today

The regular Google results page for a dental search looks different than it did a year ago. We ran the searches below on September 29, 2026 from a browser located in Cape Coral, on a phone and on a desktop, and captured the full page. Notes on each capture mark what sits above the map listings, how far down the page the map listings start, and whether the free map listings show a call button at all.

What the ten captures had in common. We captured five searches on a phone and the same five on a desktop. No unpaid map listing showed a call button on any of them. A paid listing sat inside the map results on nine of the ten, and paid ads sat above the map results on eight of the ten. The implant searches carried the most advertising: four advertisers, against none to three on the general dentist searches.

Where Call buttons do appear. Some searches show practice cards with Call, Website and Directions buttons near the top of the page. On the searches we captured, every one of those cards sat under the "Sponsored" label. On one search, "top dentist cape coral", Google also replaced the usual map results with an AI-written list of practices, and that list had no call buttons either.

How to read these. The browser's location was set at the office, and a single search is a snapshot. These captures are here to show how the page is laid out: what a searcher has to pass before reaching the profile, and what they can tap when they get there.

Our own clients: a headwind, not a rule. We checked calls from the Google profile for every single-location S8E8 client with a full record for both summers, June to August 2025 against June to August 2026. Of 38 practices, 25 had fewer calls and 13 had more. The typical change was about 9 percent down. Kari Mann's was 25 percent down.

Two things follow. And Kari Mann's drop is larger than the typical one, so the trend explains part of it, not all of it. The goal is to move this practice into the group that is growing.

Implant searches: when they fell, and where they are now

Google also reports which searches caused the profile to be shown, month by month. Kari's goal is more implant and All-on-X patients, so this is the part of the profile data that matters most to her. Searches for the practice by name are left out of the chart, because the profile's name itself contains the words "dental implants".

When it fell: before the new site, and before we started. May 2025 was a one-month spike (369), well above anything before or after it. Outside that month, implant searches showed the profile about 50 times a month in spring and summer 2025, about 20 a month in September and October 2025, and from November 2025 through June 2026 no implant search reached 15 in a month. The new site launched on May 29, 2026.

Where it is now. July 2026, the second month after launch, had 76: the highest month on record outside the May 2025 spike. August had nine different implant searches showing the profile, each under 15, the most variety since August 2025. It is two months, so it is a start and not yet a trend.

A correction to how this usually gets summarized. Adding up January to August makes 2025 look far stronger than it was, because more than half of that total came from May 2025 alone. The month-by-month view is the fair one.

How I would think about it

Two different things are being called "the decline", and they need to stay separate. The first is how often the profile is shown on the regular Google results page. That fell in November 2025, and we can show what changed on Google's results page that same month. The second is calls from the profile, which held until April and have been lower since May. We can describe that one accurately, but we cannot yet say what caused it. If the two get blended on the call, the part we can document ends up sounding like an excuse for the part we can't.

The profile is one door into the practice, not the whole building. Kari's own scoreboard is points of contact: the phone rings, or someone requests an appointment. Profile interactions are one source feeding that number. They are worth watching, but a change in this one source is not the same thing as a change in her total. Her tracking sheet is the right scoreboard, and this is one line on it.

Call buttons do not always appear on the free listing. In the searches we captured, the Call buttons sat on paid placements, and the practice was not in any of them. The free listing still showed the practice, its rating and a review, but a searcher had to tap into it to call. That is not true of every search, so it should be described as something that often happens, not something that always does. For dental searches it also goes back to 2023, and Google has never commented on it, so it should not be offered as the reason anything changed this year.

On her actual goal, the direction since launch is up. Implant searches stopped showing the profile in meaningful numbers in November 2025. July 2026 was the best implant month on record outside one spike, and August showed more variety. That is the most useful thing in this whole record for Kari, and it is measured in her own profile's numbers.

What we control and what we don't. We control how complete and accurate the profile is, where it links and which services it names. The practice controls the flow of new reviews. Nobody but Google controls the page layout or the ads above the map results.

What we do not know yet. Why calls from the profile stepped down in May. We can describe it exactly, and we will keep reporting it monthly, but we should not offer a cause we cannot support.

  • The drop in views started in November 2025, before we began and before the new site, and it matches the month paid listings started appearing inside the map results.

  • We are giving her priority services their own section on the homepage, because that is the page her Google profile sends people to. Say this once dev and design have confirmed it.
Avoid
  • Arguing that her 261 is wrong. It is real. It is partial, and it is one source.
  • Promising a date or a number of calls.
  • Naming a cause for the May change in calls. We do not have one we can support.
  • "It is bad for everyone." A third of our practices grew their profile calls this year.
  • Using any single search as proof, hers or ours, in either direction.
  • "We will optimize the profile." It is already built out. Say specifically what is being added.

The one part that sits with the practice: reviews

Google weighs recent reviews far more than the lifetime total. The practice has 895 reviews at 5.0 and a steady flow, which is worth protecting. Written reviews that mention the treatment and the area the patient came from, with a reply to each, are the ones Google shows under the listing. The implant capture above shows Google doing exactly that with one of theirs.
